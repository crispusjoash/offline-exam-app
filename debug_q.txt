
--- Q5 ---
The correct answer is **on**.

**Detailed Analysis of the Correct Answer:**
In English grammar, prepositions of transport have fixed rules. We say **by car**, **by bus**, **by train**, or **by plane** when referring to vehicles. However, when talking about walking, the only correct idiomatic expression is strictly **on foot**.

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:**
* **by:** Used for vehicles and mechanical modes of transport (e.g. *by bus*, *by bicycle**), but never for walking on your feet.
* **with:** Used to show the instrument used to perform an action (e.g. *cut with a knife**), not for walking.
* **in:** Used for being inside enclosed transport (e.g. *in a car*, *in a taxi**).

📌 **Key Learning Note:**
Always say **"on foot"** when referring to walking to school or any destination.

--- Q16 ---
The correct answer is **her**.

**Detailed Analysis of the Correct Answer:**
Possessive adjectives show ownership before a noun. Since Asha is a female person (singular female subject pronoun *she**), the corresponding possessive adjective for her grandfather is **her** (**her grandfather**).

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:**
* **his:** Possessive adjective used for a male person (e.g. *his grandfather**).
* **hers:** Possessive pronoun used without a noun following it (e.g. *the garden is hers**), whereas here it precedes the noun *grandfather*.
* **him:** Object pronoun (e.g. *I called him**), not a possessive adjective.

📌 **Key Learning Note:**
Use **her** as a possessive adjective before a noun when referring to a female person (e.g. *her book*, *her garden*, *her grandfather**).

When expressing expectations about an upcoming time period in English, we use the adjective **next**. Therefore, "sell her harvest **next month**" correctly refers to the month immediately following the present one.

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:**
* **future:** Needs a prepositional phrase like *in the future* or an adjective like *future plans*, not *future month*.

--- Q26 ---

--- Q27 ---
The correct answer is **whose**.

**Detailed Analysis of the Correct Answer:**
Relative pronouns link clauses by indicating identity or possession:
- **whose**: Shows possession for people, animals, or objects (**the hen whose egg broke* = the egg belonging to the hen).

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:**
* **who:** Subject pronoun used for human beings (e.g. *the teacher who taught us**).
* **whom:** Object pronoun used for human beings (e.g. *the man whom I met**).
* **which:** Used for non-possessive references to animals or things (e.g. *the hen which laid an egg**).

📌 **Key Learning Note:**
Use **whose** to show possession for people, animals, or things.
